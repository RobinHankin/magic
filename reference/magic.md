# Creates magic squares

Creates normal magic squares of any order \\\>2\\. Uses the appropriate
method depending on n modulo 4.

## Usage

``` r
magic(n)
```

## Arguments

- n:

  Order of magic square. If a vector, return a list whose \\i\\-th
  element is a magic square of order `n[i]`

## Details

Calls either
[`magic.2np1()`](https://robinhankin.github.io/magic/reference/magic.2np1.md),
[`magic.4n()`](https://robinhankin.github.io/magic/reference/magic.4n.md),
or
[`magic.4np2()`](https://robinhankin.github.io/magic/reference/magic.4np2.md)
depending on the value of `n`. Returns a magic square in standard format
(compare the
[`magic.2np1()`](https://robinhankin.github.io/magic/reference/magic.2np1.md)
et seq, which return the square as generated by the direct algorithm).

## References

William H. Benson and Oswald Jacoby. *New recreations with magic
squares*. Dover 1976.

## Examples

``` r
magic(6)
#>      [,1] [,2] [,3] [,4] [,5] [,6]
#> [1,]    7    6   35   34   15   14
#> [2,]    8    5   33   36   16   13
#> [3,]   27   26   19   18   11   10
#> [4,]   25   28   20   17    9   12
#> [5,]   23   22    3    2   31   30
#> [6,]   21   24    1    4   29   32
all(is.magic(magic(3:10)))
#> [1] TRUE

## The first eigenvalue of a magic square is equal to the magic constant:
eigen(magic(10),FALSE,TRUE)$values[1] - magic.constant(10)
#> [1] -4.547474e-13

## The sum of the eigenvalues of a magic square after the first is zero:
sum(eigen(magic(10),FALSE,TRUE)$values[2:10])
#> [1] 3.026023e-13


```
